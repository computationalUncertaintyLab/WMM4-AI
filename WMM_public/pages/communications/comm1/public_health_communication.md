## Detailed communication

We want to share what we know now about this outbreak, what remains uncertain, and what you can do today to protect yourself and the people around you.

An active infectious disease outbreak is ongoing in this community. Based on the contact network provided, transmission events were recorded from **March 24, 2026, through April 2, 2026**. The outbreak appears to have started with an infection of **thm220** from **exp626** on **March 24 at 9:28**, followed by rapid spread through multiple connected people over the following days. The data show many successful transmission events, including several people who then infected others, which means the outbreak is still capable of growing if transmission continues.

What we know from the dataset:
- The network contains **256 recorded contact events**.
- Of those, **204 were successful infections** and **52 were unsuccessful contacts**.
- Transmission occurred over at least **10 calendar days**, from **March 24 to April 2**.
- Some people appear to have infected many others after becoming infected themselves. Based on the recorded events, individuals with especially high numbers of successful transmissions include:
  - **abn226** with **40** recorded successful transmissions
  - **edj227** with **25**
  - **thm220** with **14**
  - **mav228** with **10**
  - **sab926** with **10**
  - **mil426** with **10**
  - **jek227** with **9**
  - **lom226** with **9**
  - **tbp226** with **9**
- Some infections happened after one or more unsuccessful contacts with the same person. This means a failed exposure at one point did **not** always prevent later infection.

What we do **not** know from these data alone:
- We do not know the name of the disease, how severe illness may be, or which symptoms to watch for.
- We do not know the exact setting of transmission, such as household, school, workplace, or social gathering.
- We do not know whether some infections were detected late or whether all contacts are captured in the dataset.
- We do not know whether any prevention steps—such as testing, isolation, masks, treatment, or vaccination—are already in place.

Because this is an evolving outbreak, we do not have all the answers yet. What is clear is that transmission is ongoing and that several people in the network spread infection to multiple others in a short period. That means early action matters.

What you can do now:
1. **Limit close contact if you feel sick.** If you have symptoms of illness, stay home and avoid exposing others until you have guidance from a healthcare provider or local public health officials.
2. **Watch for updates from trusted local health sources.** As more information becomes available about symptoms, testing, treatment, or prevention, follow that guidance promptly.
3. **Tell recent close contacts if you become ill or test positive.** People are more likely to act when information comes quickly from someone they know.
4. **Take everyday infection-control steps.** Improve ventilation when possible, cover coughs and sneezes, wash hands regularly, and avoid close contact with people who are sick.
5. **Prepare for more specific guidance.** Depending on what is learned next, public health recommendations could include testing, staying home after exposure, or avoiding certain settings for a period of time.

If you are hearing rumors, please be cautious. In outbreaks, incomplete information can spread faster than facts. The network data confirm that transmission is occurring, but they do not tell us everything about risk, severity, or where spread is most likely. We will need additional epidemiologic and clinical information to answer those questions.

We recognize that this kind of news can be stressful. It is normal to feel worried, frustrated, or uncertain when an outbreak is unfolding and information is still incomplete. The most helpful thing people can do right now is take practical steps to reduce spread, look out for one another, and rely on updated information from credible public health sources. As more is confirmed, guidance should become more specific.
