## Detailed communication

**Public Health Update: Ongoing Infectious Disease Outbreak**

We know many people may be worried about their health, their families, school, work, and daily routines. It is stressful to hear that an outbreak is continuing over time, and it is normal to feel concerned, frustrated, or uncertain. We want to share clearly what the data show, what they do not show, and what you can do right now to help reduce further spread.

### What has changed across the three data snapshots

We reviewed **three network exports**:

- **2026-04-01T2051_export.csv**  
- **2026-04-10T13-05_export.csv**  
- **2026-04-20T14-18_export.csv**

Across these three snapshots, the outbreak did **not** stop after the earlier reports. Instead, additional successful infections continued to appear in each newer file.

### What we know now from the data

Based on the network event data provided, we can say:

- The earliest successful infection in the records occurred on **March 24, 2026**.
- The newest successful infection in the most recent snapshot occurred on **April 20, 2026**.
- That means successful transmission appears in the data across at least **28 calendar days**.
- The outbreak involves **multiple chains of person-to-person spread**, not a single isolated event.
- New successful infections were still being added after the first snapshot and again after the second snapshot.
- Several infected people appear to have infected **more than one other person**, which means the outbreak can continue through branching transmission chains.
- The data contain both **successful** and **unsuccessful** contact events, showing that **not every contact led to infection**.
- The newest snapshot shows continued expansion into additional people and newer branches of the network, including transmission events recorded in mid-to-late April.

### Why this matters

An outbreak that continues across many days and many transmission chains can keep growing if people who are infected, exposed, or feeling ill continue close contact with others. Even though not every exposure results in infection, the pattern in these records shows enough successful spread to support continued transmission.

### What remains uncertain

The files provided include only these fields:

- **Actor**
- **Audience**
- **success**
- **timestamp**

Because of that, these data alone do **not** tell us:

- the name of the disease
- how severe illness may be
- what symptoms to watch for
- how the infection spreads biologically
- whether people can spread it before symptoms
- how long someone remains contagious
- which settings are highest risk
- whether testing, treatment, vaccines, masking, isolation, or other interventions are available or were already used
- whether some groups face higher medical risk than others

We want to be honest about that uncertainty. At the same time, uncertainty is not a reason to wait. It is a reason to take practical precautions now while more information is gathered.

### What the three snapshots suggest about the outbreak pattern

Looking across all three exports, the pattern suggests:

- transmission continued well beyond the first and second reports
- people infected earlier later appeared as actors in newer transmission chains
- some transmission branches extended into new groups over time
- repeated contact attempts sometimes occurred before a successful transmission
- spread remained active into **April 19 and April 20** in the newest file

This is consistent with an outbreak that is still active and still capable of growing.

### What public health is doing

Based on CERC principles, we want to explain not only what is unknown, but also the process underway. We are continuing to review the newest network data, compare changes across snapshots, identify ongoing transmission chains, and look for where spread is continuing over time. As more information becomes available, public health guidance should become more specific. We will continue to update the public with what is known, what is still uncertain, and what actions are recommended.

### What you should do right now

Please take these steps now:

1. **Stay home if you are sick.** Do not attend work, school, gatherings, or nonessential activities if you feel unwell.
2. **Reduce close contact if you think you were exposed.** If you learn you may have had contact with someone involved in this outbreak, limit contact with others.
3. **Seek medical or public health guidance promptly** if you become ill or believe you were exposed.
4. **Notify recent close contacts** if appropriate and if you are told you may be part of this outbreak, so they can take protective steps.
5. **Use extra caution in crowded or group settings** while transmission is continuing.
6. **Follow local health guidance** about testing, isolation, evaluation, or return to work or school if that guidance is available where you live.
7. **Keep a private record of recent close contacts** in case health officials ask about possible exposure.
8. **Share confirmed information only** and avoid rumors, screenshots, or naming individuals.

### If you think you were exposed

If you believe you had close contact with someone involved in this outbreak:

- reduce close contact with others as much as possible
- monitor yourself for illness
- contact a healthcare provider or public health authority for next steps
- be ready to share when the contact occurred and who was involved, privately with appropriate health personnel
- do not assume that feeling well means there is no risk, because these data do not show when infectiousness begins

### Guidance for households and families

We understand that many people are most concerned about protecting loved ones and keeping their household functioning. Families can prepare now by:

- making a plan for how a sick person could stay apart from others if needed
- arranging help with food, medications, childcare, classes, or errands
- checking in on relatives, roommates, neighbors, and others who may need support
- planning for missed work, school, or caregiving responsibilities
- using calm, factual information from trusted sources

### Guidance for students, workers, and community members

You can help reduce spread by:

- not going to class or work when sick
- reporting illness or exposure through appropriate channels if asked
- preparing for schedule disruptions
- helping others who need to stay home
- avoiding blame, gossip, or public sharing of names

### Guidance for community organizations

Schools, employers, faith communities, and local organizations should:

- share timely updates in plain language
- encourage people to stay home when sick
- make it easier for people to seek guidance
- protect privacy
- prepare for possible disruptions if additional cases occur
- support people who may need to miss class, work, events, or services

### Privacy, stigma, and respect

People involved in an outbreak should not be blamed, targeted, or publicly identified. Stigma can make people less likely to seek help or share important information, which can worsen spread. Please focus on prevention, support, and facts. Respecting privacy helps the whole community respond more effectively.

### Our commitment

This is an evolving situation, and we do not yet have all the answers. What we can say clearly from the three data snapshots is:

- the outbreak is still active
- successful transmission appears from **March 24 through April 20, 2026**
- spread has continued across multiple chains over time
- community action now can help reduce further transmission

### Key points to remember

- This outbreak remains active in the newest data.
- Successful infections were recorded from **March 24 to April 20, 2026**.
- The outbreak is spreading through multiple person-to-person chains.
- Not every contact leads to infection, but enough do that continued spread remains a concern.
- Stay home if sick, reduce close contact if exposed, seek guidance, and avoid rumors and stigma.
