## Detailed communication

**Public Health Update: Ongoing Infectious Disease Outbreak**

We know many people are worried about their own health, their family members, classes, work, and daily routines. It is also stressful to hear that an outbreak is still spreading days after it first began. We want to be clear about what we know from the network event data, what we do not yet know, and what actions people can take right now to reduce further spread.

### What has changed since the earlier snapshot

We reviewed **two outbreak snapshots**:

- an earlier export dated **2026-04-01T2051**
- a newer export dated **2026-04-10T13-05**

The newer file shows that transmission did **not stop** after the earlier report. Instead, new successful infections continued after April 2 and were still being recorded through **April 10, 2026**. That means this outbreak remains active based on the data provided.

### What we know now

From the two network exports, we can say the following:

- The earliest successful infection in the data occurred on **March 24, 2026**.
- The newest successful infection in the current snapshot occurred on **April 10, 2026**.
- This means the outbreak has shown transmission over at least **18 calendar days** in the data provided.
- The outbreak involves **multiple chains of person-to-person spread**, not a single isolated event.
- New infections continued to appear in the newer snapshot after the first snapshot ended.
- Several infected people appear to have transmitted infection to more than one other person, which means spread can continue through branching chains.
- The data include both **successful** and **unsuccessful** contact events, so not every contact resulted in infection.
- The newer snapshot also suggests the outbreak network is still expanding into additional people and chains of transmission.

### Why this matters

An outbreak that continues across many days and multiple transmission chains can keep growing if exposed or sick people continue close contact with others. Even though not every contact leads to infection, the current pattern shows enough successful transmission to support ongoing spread.

### What we do **not** know from these data

The files you provided contain only:

- **Actor**
- **Audience**
- **success**
- **timestamp**

Because of that, we **cannot** say from these data alone:

- the name of the disease
- how severe illness is
- what symptoms to watch for
- how the infection spreads biologically
- whether people are contagious before symptoms
- how long people remain contagious
- which settings are highest risk
- whether any treatment or vaccine is available
- whether children, older adults, pregnant people, or immunocompromised people face different risks
- whether isolation, testing, masking, or other interventions were already in place

We want to be honest: there is still important uncertainty. But uncertainty is not a reason to do nothing. It is a reason to take practical steps now while more information is gathered.

### What the two snapshots suggest about the outbreak pattern

Comparing the earlier and newer exports shows that:

- transmission continued **well beyond** the first reported window
- the outbreak moved through **new branches** of the contact network
- some people infected in earlier chains later appear as actors in newer chains
- repeated contact attempts sometimes occurred before a successful transmission

This is consistent with an outbreak that requires continued vigilance and prompt action.

### What you can do right now

Please take these steps now to protect yourself and others:

1. **Stay home if you are sick.** Avoid work, school, gatherings, and nonessential social contact if you feel unwell.
2. **Reduce close contact if you think you were exposed.** If you learn that you may have had contact with someone in this outbreak, limit contact with others while you seek guidance.
3. **Seek medical or public health guidance promptly** if you become ill or believe you were exposed.
4. **Notify recent close contacts** if you are told you may be part of this outbreak, so they can take steps to protect themselves and others.
5. **Use extra caution in group settings and crowded indoor spaces** while transmission is ongoing.
6. **Follow any instructions from your local health authority** about testing, isolation, evaluation, or return to school or work.
7. **Rely on confirmed information** and avoid spreading rumors, names, screenshots, or speculation.

### If you think you were exposed

If you think you had close contact with someone involved in this outbreak:

- reduce close contact with others as much as you can
- monitor yourself for illness
- contact a healthcare provider or public health authority for next steps
- be ready to share **when the contact occurred** and **who was involved**, privately and only with appropriate health personnel
- avoid assuming that feeling well means there is no risk, because these data do not tell us when infectiousness begins

### Guidance for households and families

We know people are especially concerned about protecting children, older adults, and family members who may need extra support. Even though these data do not tell us which groups are at highest medical risk, families can still prepare now:

- make a plan for how a sick person could stay apart from others if needed
- arrange help for food, medications, childcare, or errands
- check in on older relatives, people with disabilities, and others who may need assistance
- keep a record of important contacts and recent close interactions
- share updates calmly and use trusted sources

### Guidance for students, workers, and community members

Whether you are attending classes, working, caregiving, or living with roommates, you can help reduce spread by:

- not attending class or work when sick
- reporting illness or exposure through appropriate channels if asked
- planning ahead for missed responsibilities
- supporting others who need to stay home
- avoiding stigma, gossip, or blame

### Guidance for community organizations

Schools, employers, faith groups, clubs, and community organizations should:

- share timely, plain-language updates
- encourage people to stay home when sick
- make it easier for people to seek health guidance
- avoid identifying affected people publicly
- prepare for possible disruptions if additional cases occur
- provide respectful support for people who may need to miss work, class, or events

### A note on privacy, respect, and stigma

People involved in an outbreak should not be blamed, singled out, or harassed. Public sharing of names, rumors, or guesses can cause harm and may make people less likely to come forward. Outbreak response works better when people are treated with respect and supported in taking protective action.

### Our commitment

This is an evolving situation. We do not yet have all the answers, and we will not guess or overstate what these data can show. What we can say clearly is this:

- the outbreak is ongoing
- transmission continued after the first snapshot
- successful infections were still recorded through **April 10, 2026**
- community action now can help slow further spread

### Key points to remember

- This outbreak is still active in the newest data.
- Transmission spans from **March 24 to April 10, 2026** in the records provided.
- Spread is occurring through multiple chains of person-to-person infection.
- Not every contact causes infection, but enough do that continued spread remains a concern.
- Stay home if sick, reduce contact if exposed, seek guidance, and avoid rumors and stigma.
